# The Interpreter

A court interpreter is hired to be nobody: to carry words across
and add nothing of her own. In this poem she keeps the form of the
job and changes one word inside it — "he did it" becomes "he was
there" — and the sentence, in both senses, changes. Nobody in the
room can check her, because nobody else speaks her language. Then
she takes off her headphones and goes home to make dinner, as if
nothing happened.

The poem is about agency held by the person a room agrees has none:
the power to act hides in the one seam no one is watching.
